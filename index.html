About Me

Welcome! I am a second-year Ph.D. student at the University of Georgia with a passion for plant biology, genetics, and bioinformatics. My research focuses on understanding how genetic variation contributes to plant traits and evolution, using computational approaches to explore complex genomic data. Beyond research, I am passionate about teaching, mentorship, and creating opportunities for students to engage in scientific discovery. My long-term goal is to become a biology professor at a primarily undergraduate institution, where I can combine research and teaching to inspire the next generation of scientists. I believe some of the most rewarding moments in science come from asking questions, exploring new ideas, and sharing the excitement of discovery with others.

Research Interests

My research explores how differences in DNA influence the traits and evolution of morning glories (Ipomoea). Using DNA sequencing and computer-based tools, I study how changes in a plant's genetic code can lead to differences in characteristics such as flower size, leaf shape, and growth. By comparing the DNA of different morning glory plants, I hope to better understand how plants develop their unique traits and how they change over time. This research can help us better understand plant diversity, improve our knowledge of important crop relatives, and uncover the genetic changes behind traits that make each plant unique.
