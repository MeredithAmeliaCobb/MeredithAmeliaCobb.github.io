Why I Teach

As an educator, I strive to inspire students to become lifelong learners who remain curious, explore their interests, and adapt as they encounter new ideas and challenges.

My Teaching Philosophy

I approach teaching through three central ideas:

Learning Is a Lifelong Process

I believe that learning never stops, and I strive to bring that mindset into the classroom. Teaching is not simply a one-way transfer of knowledge, but an opportunity for both students and instructors to learn and grow. One of the most rewarding parts of teaching is watching students discover their own interests, ask new questions, and experience that “light bulb” moment when a challenging concept finally clicks. At the same time, teaching provides me with the opportunity to continue learning by exploring new ways to present material, engage students, and spark interest in scientific topics.

Learning Is an Exploratory Process

My approach to teaching focuses on helping students develop both a strong understanding of biological concepts and the ability to think critically about scientific questions. I incorporate real-world examples, research, and problem-solving activities to help students explor the topic more deaply. I enjoy creating an interactive classroom environment where students are encouraged to participate. I regularly pause to ask questions, encourage discussion, and check for understanding. I find that visual explanations, including drawing diagrams and working through processes on the board, can be especially helpful when introducing complex biological concepts. My goal is to make challenging material approachable while giving students the confidence to think through problems and explor the topics on their own.

Learning is an Adaptive Process

I believe mistakes are an important part of learning and that students should have opportunities to practice, make mistakes, and improve along the way. I value effort, perseverance, and growth, recognizing that every student comes into the classroom with different experiences, strengths, and learning needs. My goal is to create an environment where students feel encouraged to keep working through challenges rather than becoming discouraged when something does not come easily.

I also believe that effective teaching requires a willingness to continue adapting my own methods. As new technologies and approaches to education emerge, I see opportunities to rethink how course material is presented and how students engage with it. I want to help prepare students for a changing scientific world by encouraging them to use emerging technologies thoughtfully, critically, and responsibly.

Above all, I strive to create an inclusive and supportive learning environment where all students feel valued and capable of success. My goal is for students to leave my classroom not only with a stronger understanding of biology, but with increased confidence in their ability to think critically, solve problems, and pursue their own academic and professional goals. Ultimately, I aim to empower students to see themselves as scientists and lifelong learners.

Growing the Next Generation of Scientists

Teaching extends beyond the classroom. I enjoy helping students develop confidence in their abilities, become comfortable asking questions, and learn how to approach unfamiliar problems.

Through mentoring and undergraduate research experiences, I hope to encourage students to become independent thinkers who are confident exploring scientific questions of their own. Providing opportunities for students to participate in research is especially important to me because it allows them to experience the process of scientific discovery firsthand.
